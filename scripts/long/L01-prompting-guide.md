## Script

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "You're using AI wrong." Fast zoom-in]**
VO: You're probably using AI wrong. Not because you're bad at it — but because nobody ever taught you how to talk to it.

**[SCREEN: REC — typing "write a blog post about fitness" into a chatbot, generic answer appears]**
VO: Most people type something like this. "Write a blog post about fitness." And the AI gives back something bland, generic, and honestly… useless. So they decide AI is overrated.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "Same AI. Better prompt."]**
VO: But the exact same AI, with a better prompt, gives you something you could actually publish. By the end of this video, you'll have a simple five-part framework and four pro techniques that work in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — any chatbot. And I'll give you a copy-paste template at the end.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "R · T · C · F · E" each letter appearing]**
VO: Here's the framework. Five letters: R, T, C, F, E. Role, Task, Context, Format, and Examples. Let's go through each one.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "1. ROLE" / AI-IMG — "a glowing silhouette wearing different hats: chef, doctor, coach, dark tech background"]**
VO: Number one: Role. Tell the AI who it should be. "You are an experienced fitness coach who writes for complete beginners." This one sentence changes the vocabulary, the depth, and the tone of everything that follows. An AI pretending to be a coach writes differently from an AI pretending to be a scientist.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "2. TASK — one clear verb"]**
VO: Number two: Task. Use one clear action verb. Write, summarize, compare, rewrite, plan. Not "can you help me with something about fitness?" Instead: "Write a 600-word blog post."

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "3. CONTEXT = the secret ingredient"]**
VO: Number three — and this is the one almost everyone skips — Context. Who is this for? Why does it matter? What do they already know? For example: "The readers are busy parents in their thirties who haven't exercised in years and only have twenty minutes a day." Suddenly the AI knows exactly who it's talking to. The more context you give, the less generic the answer.

**[SCREEN: REC — scrolling a response that is a neat table and bullet list]**
VO: Number four: Format. Tell it what the output should look like. A table. Five bullet points. A script with timestamps. An email under 150 words. If you don't say, the AI guesses — and it usually guesses "long wall of text."

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "5. EXAMPLES"]**
VO: Number five: Examples. If you have a piece of writing you like, paste it in and say "match this style." AI is incredible at copying patterns. One good example beats ten adjectives.

**[SCREEN: REC — typing the full structured prompt, then the much better answer appearing]**
VO: Put it together: "You are an experienced fitness coach who writes for beginners. Write a 600-word blog post about starting exercise. The readers are busy parents in their thirties with only twenty minutes a day. Use short paragraphs, three subheadings, and end with a simple one-week plan in a table." Look at the difference. Same AI. Completely different result.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "PRO TECHNIQUES" with a level-up sound]**
VO: That framework alone puts you ahead of most users. But here are four techniques the pros use.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "Technique 1: Show, don't tell"]**
VO: Technique one: give examples of the output. If you want product descriptions, show it two you like, then ask for a third. This is called few-shot prompting, and it's the fastest way to get consistent results — especially for anything you do over and over.

**[SCREEN: REC — prompt "Before you start, ask me 5 questions…" and the AI asking questions]**
VO: Technique two: make the AI interview you. Add this line to the end of any prompt: "Before you start, ask me any questions you need to do this well." Now instead of guessing, the AI asks what it's missing. You answer, and the result is tailored to you. This one trick fixes most bad answers.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "Think step by step"]**
VO: Technique three: ask for step-by-step reasoning. For anything with logic — math, planning, decisions, comparing options — say: "Think through this step by step before giving your final answer." Many AI tools now have a dedicated reasoning or "thinking" mode too. Use it for hard problems, and skip it for simple ones, because it's slower.

**[SCREEN: REC — prompt "Critique your answer as a strict editor, then rewrite it"]**
VO: Technique four: make it critique itself. After any answer, say: "Act as a strict editor. List the three biggest weaknesses in what you just wrote, then rewrite it fixing them." The second version is almost always better. You're basically getting a free second draft.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "Common mistakes" with ❌ icons]**
VO: Quick warnings. Don't paste private information — passwords, client data, ID numbers — into any AI tool. Always double-check facts, numbers and quotes, because AI can sound confident and still be wrong. And don't restart a new chat every time; in the same chat, the AI remembers what you've already told it.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — full template on screen, highlighted line by line]**
VO: Here's the master template. Pause the video and screenshot this.
"You are a [role]. Your task is to [verb + deliverable]. The audience is [who], and they [what they know or need]. Format it as [format and length]. Here's an example of the style I want: [example]. Before you start, ask me any questions you need."

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "Save this. Use it daily."]**
VO: Save that template and use it for everything — emails, study notes, social posts, business plans.
