## Script

**[SCREEN: REC — dozens of browser tabs open, fast scroll]**
VO: Forty tabs open. Three hours gone. And you still don't really understand the topic. Sound familiar?

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "4 steps. 1 hour."]**
VO: Research doesn't have to be like this. I'm going to show you a four-step AI workflow that turns hours of research into about one hour — and I'll show you how to avoid the mistakes that make AI research dangerous. This works whether you're a student, a freelancer, or researching a business idea.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "STEP 1: MAP THE TOPIC"]**
VO: Step one: map the topic before you read anything. Most people jump straight into articles. Instead, ask an AI chatbot for the map.

**[SCREEN: REC — typing the prompt below, AI returning a structured outline]**
VO: Use this prompt: "I'm new to [topic]. Give me a map of it: the five to seven key sub-topics, the important terms I need to know, the main debates or disagreements, and the questions an expert would ask." In thirty seconds you have the shape of the whole subject. Now you know what you're looking for — and what you don't know yet.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "This is a MAP, not the truth"]**
VO: Important: treat this as a map, not as facts. We'll verify everything in the next step.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "STEP 2: FIND REAL SOURCES"]**
VO: Step two: find real sources. Use an AI search tool — something like Perplexity, or the web-search mode in ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. These search the internet and show you the links they used.

**[SCREEN: REC — AI search answer with numbered citations, cursor clicking a citation]**
VO: Search each sub-topic from your map. And here's the key habit: click the citations. Open the actual sources. You're looking for official websites, research papers, reputable news, and experts — not random blogs that copied each other.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "Save 5–10 best sources"]**
VO: Collect the five to ten best sources. Save the PDFs and copy the links. Quality over quantity.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "STEP 3: CHAT WITH YOUR SOURCES"]**
VO: Step three is where the real time-saving happens: chat with your sources. Tools like Google's NotebookLM — or uploading files into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini — let you upload your PDFs, articles and notes, and then ask questions answered *only* from those documents.

**[SCREEN: REC — uploading PDFs into a notebook tool, asking a question, answer with source references]**
VO: Ask things like: "Summarize the main argument of each source." "Where do these sources disagree?" "What evidence supports this claim?" "Explain this section like I'm fifteen." Because the answers are grounded in your documents — often with references you can click — this is much more reliable than asking an AI from memory.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "Bonus: audio overviews"]**
VO: Bonus: some of these tools can turn your sources into a podcast-style audio summary. Listen to it on a walk or a commute, and you're learning while doing something else.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "STEP 4: MAKE IT YOURS"]**
VO: Step four: turn it into your own notes. This step is where actual learning happens — and where you stop being a copy-paste machine.

**[SCREEN: REC — prompt "Quiz me with 10 questions…" and AI quizzing]**
VO: Write a short summary in your own words first. Then paste it into the AI and say: "Act as an expert. What did I get wrong or miss?" Then: "Quiz me with ten questions on this topic, one at a time." If you can answer them, you understand it. If not, you know exactly what to review.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "3 RULES" with shield icon]**
VO: Now the three rules that keep you from getting fooled.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "1. Verify every number, quote, and date"]**
VO: Rule one: verify every number, quote and date against an original source. AI can invent statistics that sound completely real.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "2. Check the date"]**
VO: Rule two: check how recent the information is. AI tools may be working from older data, and in fast-moving topics, last year's facts can already be wrong.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — "3. You're responsible, not the AI"]**
VO: Rule three: you are responsible for what you publish or submit, not the AI. If you're a student, check your school's rules on AI use. If it's for a client, make sure the final work is accurate.

**[SCREEN: TEXT — recap list: Map → Sources → Chat → Own it]**
VO: Quick recap. Map the topic. Find real sources. Chat with your sources. Make it your own. Four steps, and that forty-tab nightmare becomes a focused one-hour session.

**[SCREEN: end screen]**
VO: If you want better answers in every one of these steps, watch my prompting guide next — it's the foundation for everything on this channel. See you there.
