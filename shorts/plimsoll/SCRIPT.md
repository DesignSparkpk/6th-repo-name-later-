## Narration v2 (55 s target: ~199 words at the narrator's 3.65 words/s)
1. He wanted one line painted on every ship, and it nearly got him thrown out of Parliament.
2. London, the 1870s. Shipowners have a trick.
3. Insure an old, rotting ship for more than it's worth, then cram it with so much cargo it sits almost underwater.
4. If it floats, they get rich. If it sinks, they get richer.
5. Sailors call them coffin ships.
6. Then an MP named Samuel Plimsoll has an idea that sounds like a joke. Paint a line on the hull. Water over the line, the ship stays home.
7. The shipowners hate it, and plenty of them sit in Parliament.
8. So when the government drops the bill, Plimsoll snaps. He shakes his fist and calls them villains. Told to take it back, he refuses.
9. The papers print every word. One crowd says if he's mad, he should bite a few more MPs.
10. Wait. Did it work?
11. Kind of. In 1876 the line became law, but the shipowners got to decide where to paint it.
12. Years later, the government finally fixed its spot. Now it's on cargo ships worldwide.
13. And those rubber gym shoes? The stripe around the sole looked so much like his line, they were named after it. Plimsolls.

## Beat map
- Hook: the stakes (nearly thrown out of Parliament) plus an odd, tiny thing (one painted line).
- Scam escalation (3-5): rich if it floats, richer if it sinks. Mockery (6): "sounds like a joke". Climax (9-10): the fist, "villains", "bite a few more MPs".
- Rehook: "Wait. Did it work?" A twist: owners painted it wherever they liked. Payoff that loops into everyday life: the shoes.

## Visual moods
Foggy grey docks, then a rotting ship sinking under cargo at a stormy night sea, then warm candle-lit Commons with green benches,
then newspaper print and a crowd, then a gloomy owner painting the line in a silly spot, then a sunny modern container port,
then a bright school gym with plimsolls (the final shot).

## Facts and sources
- Coffin ships were overloaded and over-insured, so owners profited even when they sank: 99% Invisible ep. 33; National Museums Liverpool; Wikipedia.
- 22 July 1875: Plimsoll shook his fist, called members "villains" and refused to withdraw the word when the Speaker asked;
  the papers printed his protest. Sheffield meeting quote: "If Mr Plimsoll is mad, the sooner he bit a few other MPs the better." (National Museums Liverpool)
- Disraeli announced the bill would not pass before the recess (Liverpool; Wikipedia). Disraeli moved a reprimand, adjourned; Plimsoll later apologised (Wikipedia).
- The 1876 Act made load lines compulsory, but owners set the line themselves; its position was fixed by law later
  (1890 or 1894 by source, so the script says only "years"): Liverpool; Royal Museums Greenwich.
- Plimsoll shoes were named in the 1920s for their resemblance to the line (Wikipedia).
- NOT used (unverified): suspension from Parliament, a line painted on the funnel, any death toll numbers.
