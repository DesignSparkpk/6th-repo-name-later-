## 5. Facts and sources
- Coffin ships were overloaded and over-insured, so owners profited even when they sank (99% Invisible ep. 33; National Museums Liverpool; Wikipedia).
- "Plenty of them sit in Parliament": powerful shipowners, many of them MPs (History Hit).
- 22 July 1875: Disraeli announced the shipping bill would not pass before the recess. Plimsoll stamped his foot, slapped his written protest on the table,
  shook his fist, called members "villains" and refused to withdraw when the Speaker asked. Quote: "I am determined to unmask the villains that send out
  seamen to death and destruction!" His wife Eliza handed copies to reporters in the Press Gallery, and the papers printed the protest in full.
  A Sheffield meeting: "If Mr Plimsoll is mad, the sooner he bit a few other MPs the better." (National Museums Liverpool)
- A stop-gap act letting surveyors stop unseaworthy ships passed within weeks (law by August 1875; National Museums Liverpool).
- The 1876 Merchant Shipping Act made load lines compulsory (Royal Museums Greenwich).
- Plimsoll shoes were named for their resemblance to the Plimsoll line (Wikipedia).
- Dramatised (no named speaker): "A line of paint? On MY ship?" The quotes are shortened: "unmask the villains", and the crowd line is a paraphrase.
- NOT used (unverified or disputed): suspension from Parliament, a line painted on the funnel, death-toll numbers, the 1890 vs 1894 date for fixing the line's position.
