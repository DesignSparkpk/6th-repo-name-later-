## What changed from v3, and why (retention)
| Problem in v3 | Fix in v4 |
|---|---|
| The hook asked no question that only the ending answers, so viewers could leave once they got the paradox | The hook ends "...and the boss who fired him had to watch". That open loop is only paid off in the last line. |
| "The man in charge" meant nothing; viewers didn't know why the firing was unfair | Line 1: he was the man behind Ford's Mustang, and Ford fired him anyway. The outrage makes viewers want the revenge. |
| "They said yes" ended the tension at the halfway point; nothing new happened between 50 % and 80 % | A real catch: Congress said yes only if Chrysler found another $2 billion on its own. Tension continues to the payoff. |
| The ending was a statement | The ending is the loop close, on the boss watching: a visual reversal, then cut to black. |

### Hook, frame by frame (the 3 seconds that decide the swipe)
- 0.0 s: a hand slaps a single dollar bill onto a desk. Hit.
- 0.4 s: whip-pan to the factory wall. A giant red counter spins past $1,000,000,000 and red ink pours down the walls.
- 1.5 s: cars on the line stall one after another like dominoes, and a siren light spins.
- 2.5 s: crash zoom onto the man holding the dollar, deadpan, in a cheap suit.
- 4.5 s (on "the boss who fired him"): a smash cut to a dark boardroom. An old man watches a TV with his arms crossed, and the camera snaps back.

The viewer has seen the threat, the number, the paradox and a promise of revenge before the 6-second mark.

## Facts and sources (verified 2026-11-27)
- Chrysler's 1979 loss was about $1.1 billion (Congressional Research Service: $1.126 billion), called at the time the gaudiest red ink in US corporate history. (CRS R40005; TIME; EBSCO.)
- Iacocca cut his own base salary to one dollar a year. (EBSCO, the Chrysler bailout research starter.) The script says "worked for one dollar" for that period only; it does not claim he never got paid more.
- Iacocca led development of the Ford Mustang. (Britannica; Biography.com.)
- Henry Ford II fired Iacocca in July 1978. The widely reported explanation was "I just don't like you". (UPI archives; Encyclopedia.com; Biography.com.) The script shows it as "reportedly".
- Congress approved up to $1.5 billion in federal loan guarantees (signed January 1980). To qualify, Chrysler had to raise about $2 billion on its own (concessions and other financing). Critics opposed the rescue. Chrysler drew about $1.2 billion. (EBSCO; History.com; Britannica; Heritage Foundation as a critic.)
- Chrysler repaid the guaranteed loans in 1983, about seven years early. (CSM, 14 Jul 1983; TIME, "Chrysler pays off its loan".)
- 1984: a record profit of about $2.4 billion. (TIME; business and encyclopedia sources.)
- NOT used:
  - The exact day of the final repayment (sources conflict).
  - "Five years to the day".
  - Job counts (unverified and disputed).
  - Any "bankruptcy filing" claim: it was a loan guarantee, not a formal bankruptcy.
- Dramatized:
  - "The boss... had to watch" and the boardroom TV are a story device: Henry Ford II was still alive then, but there is no record of him watching anything, and the narration claims none.
  - All dialogue other than the reported line.
  - The visual metaphors: the red-ink flood, the scale, the cash pile, the cheque and the coffee cup.
  - Henry Ford II, Iacocca and the senators are generic cartoon stand-ins, not portraits. No car-brand logos are shown.
