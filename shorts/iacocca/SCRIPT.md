# "I Just Don't Like You": script v5 (~125 spoken words, ~36 s)

v5 = the owner picked hook A (the insult first, then the billion-dollar comeback promise), and the whole story is laid out on the reference pattern:
- the hook names the wrong done to him and promises the comeback;
- "You see," pivot to who he was;
- the stakes keep climbing (dying company, one-dollar salary, begging Congress, "let it die", the $2 billion catch);
- the payoff;
- the last line turns his boss's insult around.

v1-v4 are in git history.

Why this topic (vidIQ, 2026-11-27): the "fired / laughed at, then proved them wrong" format is the strongest pattern in recent Shorts (27.7M and 12.2M views). A 7-second Iacocca clip got 2.8M views from a 9.6k-subscriber channel, while the best full versions are tiny (13k): demand is proven, and nobody has told the story well.

### Hook, frame by frame (the 3 seconds that decide the swipe)
- 0.0 s: dark boardroom. An old man behind a huge desk raises one finger. The first word lands with a hit.
- 0.8 s: on "five words", five counter ticks pop on screen, 1-2-3-4-5, one per word.
- 1.5 s: "I just don't like you." The desk lamp pops and sparks.
- 2.5 s: smash cut. Two guards throw the hero out of a glass tower with a cardboard box.
- 3.8 s: whip-pan to a factory wall. A giant red counter spins past $1,000,000,000 and red ink floods down.
- 5.5 s: on "turned it around", the counter flips and starts running backwards, and the ink starts flowing back up.

By the 6-second mark the viewer has seen the insult, the stakes, the number and the promise.

## Facts and sources (verified 2026-11-27)
- Henry Ford II fired Lee Iacocca as Ford president in July 1978. The widely reported explanation was "I just don't like you" (five words). (UPI archives; Encyclopedia.com; Biography.com.) The narration says "fired him with five words"; it is a reported line.
- Iacocca led development of the Ford Mustang. (Britannica; Biography.com.)
- Chrysler lost about $1.1 billion in 1979 (Congressional Research Service: $1.126 billion), called at the time the gaudiest red ink in US corporate history. (CRS R40005; TIME; EBSCO.)
- Iacocca cut his own base salary to one dollar a year. (EBSCO, Chrysler bailout.)
- Congress approved up to $1.5 billion in federal loan guarantees, signed January 1980. Critics opposed the rescue. To qualify, Chrysler had to raise about $2 billion on its own, through concessions and other financing. Chrysler drew about $1.2 billion of the guarantee. (EBSCO; History.com; Britannica; Heritage Foundation as a critic.)
- Chrysler repaid the guaranteed loans in 1983, about seven years early. (CSM, 14 Jul 1983; TIME, "Chrysler pays off its loan".)
- 1984: a record profit of about $2.4 billion. (TIME; business and encyclopedia sources.)
- NOT used:
  - the exact day of the final repayment (sources conflict);
  - "five years to the day";
  - job counts;
  - any "bankruptcy filing" claim (it was a loan guarantee).
- Dramatized:
  - "Find two billion more on your own" is a paraphrase of the condition, not a quote.
  - The last line is the narrator's opinion.
  - The boss watching TV is a visual device; no record of it.
  - The red ink, the scale, the cash piles and the cheque are visual metaphors.
  - Henry Ford II, Iacocca and the senators are generic cartoon stand-ins, not portraits. No car-brand logos are shown.
