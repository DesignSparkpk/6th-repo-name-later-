# The Building That Melted a Car

Style: cartoon-3d-short (2D bean-head cartoon cards in a painted 3D London street, black ink lines, gliding cameras, steady narration, synthesized SFX and an original score). No captions or labels. English only.

## Facts (with sources)
- **London, 2013:** 20 Fenchurch Street, nicknamed the "Walkie-Talkie", has a concave south face that focused sunlight onto Eastcheap, the street below. A parked Jaguar XJ's wing mirror, panels and badge melted. ([National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/130904-walkie-talkie-building-london-melts-sunlight-physics-science), [Pop Sci](https://www.popsci.com/science/article/2013-09/why-do-skyscrapers-melt-things/), [ITV](https://www.itv.com/news/update/2013-09-03/glare-from-walkie-talkie-skyscraper-melts-jaguar/))
- **Other damage:** paint blistered, fabric was singed and carpets were charred in nearby shops; the City of London suspended three parking bays. ([world-architects](https://world-architects.com/en/pages/headlines/walkie-talkie-building-melts-cars), [Imperial College](https://imperial.ac.uk/news/190748/did-british-weather-save-from-walkie/amp))
- **The egg:** a City A.M. reporter fried an egg in the beam on the pavement. ([City A.M.](https://www.cityam.com/?p=550958), [Business Insider](https://businessinsider.in/the-ultra-hot-death-ray-from-londons-infamous-walkie-talkie-building-is-now-being-used-to-fry-an-egg-on-the-street/articleshow/22260719.cms))
- **The physics:** a simulation found the hot spot received about 10–15 times the heat of direct sunlight; Imperial College researchers concluded British cloud cover kept it from being worse. ([Pop Sci](https://www.popsci.com/science/article/2013-09/why-do-skyscrapers-melt-things/), [Imperial College](https://imperial.ac.uk/news/190748/did-british-weather-save-from-walkie/amp))
- **The twist:** the same architect's Vdara hotel in Las Vegas (opened 2009) focused sun onto its pool deck around 2010; guests reported singed hair and melted plastic cups and bags, and staff called it the "Vdara death ray". ([Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/2013/09/16/dailydirt-dangerous-death-ray-buildings/), [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/death-ray-holiday/))
- **Needs one more check before the voice is recorded:** the permanent sunshade fitted to the facade in 2014.

## Script (about 225 words, about 61 s at 3.7 words/s)
| Beat | Narration | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | In 2013, a skyscraper in London melted a car. From across the street. | Close-up: a car's wing mirror drooping like warm cheese. |
| Setting | It was brand new, all shiny glass, and so oddly shaped that Londoners called it the Walkie-Talkie. | Wide aerial glide over the City of London; the curved tower gleams. |
| Action 1 | But its south side curved inward. Like a giant spoon. | Low angle up the concave face; sun glints. |
| Action 2 | And on sunny afternoons, a blazing spot of light started creeping down the street below. | A bright ellipse slides along the pavement; a pigeon hops out of it. |
| Action 3 | It blistered the paint on shop fronts. It scorched a carpet. | A shopkeeper looks at a smoking doormat. |
| Action 4 | Then one man parked his Jaguar right in the spot. | The driver locks the car and walks off whistling. |
| Snap | When he came back, the mirror, the panels and the badge had melted. | He returns; the mirror slowly droops; his face falls. |
| Deadpan | Luckily, nobody was hurt. Unluckily, a reporter brought an egg. | A reporter cracks an egg onto the pavement; it sizzles. |
| Explanation | A curved mirror works like a magnifying glass for sunlight. It gathers light from a huge area and squeezes it onto one small spot. Scientists worked out that spot was getting ten to fifteen times the heat of normal sunshine. | A kid with a magnifying glass focuses a dot onto a leaf; it smokes. Then the tower's light beams converge onto the street. |
| Consequence | The city closed the parking spaces, and the owners bolted giant sunshades onto the glass. | Workers on a cradle fit long fins; the bright spot fades. |
| Twist | And the architect? He'd seen this before. Three years earlier, his curved hotel in Las Vegas melted guests' plastic cups by the pool. The staff called it the death ray. | A sunny pool deck; a plastic cup slowly folds over; a lifeguard looks up at the curved hotel. |
