# Escape Fire (draft v1): Mann Gulch, Montana, 1949
Why this topic: survival and a paradox ("set a fire to survive a fire"). No YouTube Short or TikTok on it turned up in search
(only long documentaries and a song), it is a true US story, and its ending is a big visual climax. Tone: the hero is
the misunderstood genius; the 13 who died are treated with respect, never as a joke.

## Narration (~205 words, ~60 s at 3.4 words/s)
1. To survive a wildfire racing up the hill behind him, he set the grass in front of him on fire.
2. Montana, 1949. Fifteen smokejumpers parachute into a steep gulch to fight what looks like a small fire across the valley.
3. Then the wind flips. The fire jumps the gulch and starts climbing the hill behind them, through grass as dry as paper.
4. Their foreman, Wag Dodge, yells: drop your tools and run.
5. But the fire is faster than any man can climb.
6. So with the flames right behind them, Dodge stops, pulls out a match, and lights the grass in front of him.
7. His crew stares at him like he's lost his mind. They're trapped by a fire, and he just made a second one.
8. He waves them into his burning patch. They run right past him.
9. So Dodge does something even weirder. He walks into his own fire, lies face down in the hot ashes, and waits.
10. Wait. Why would anyone lie down in a fire?
11. You see, a fire is only dangerous while it has something to eat. Dodge's little fire burned the grass around him to ash in seconds.
12. So when the giant wall of flame arrived, there was nothing left to burn where he lay. It roared around him and kept going.
13. Two of his men squeezed through a crack in the rocks at the top and made it. Thirteen didn't.
14. And Dodge stood up from the ashes, alive.
15. Today, his trick has a name. The escape fire.

## Beat map (formula check)
- Hook 0-4 s: the paradox action (lighting grass in front of the fire), with the threat visible in the same frame.
- Mockery: the crew's stares ("he just made a second one").
- Rehook 1: "So Dodge does something even weirder." Rehook 2: "Wait. Why would anyone lie down in a fire?"
- "You see," then the mechanism (no fuel, no fire). Payoff: he lives, and the trick is named. This loops back to the hook.

## Visual plan (moods change with the story)
Bright afternoon over the gulch with parachutes, then orange smoke as the wind flips, then a wall of fire climbing the slope,
then a match close-up, then a ring of black ash, then the fire wall passes over him, then dusk smoke and the rock crack at the ridge,
then calm evening with Dodge standing in the black patch. Hook = one camera journey: the match, then a pull back, then the fire wall behind him.

## Facts and sources
- Aug 5, 1949. 15 smokejumpers plus a fire guard. 13 died; 3 survived (Dodge, Sallee, Rumsey). Sources: USFS Missoula smokejumper history; Wikipedia "Mann Gulch fire".
- Dodge lit an escape fire, a technique the crew had not been trained in, and could not get the idea across to his men (Wikipedia; USFS).
- He lay face down in the ashes while the main fire passed over (Wikipedia/USFS summaries).
- Sallee and Rumsey escaped through an opening in the rocks at the ridge top (USFS).
- NOT used (unverified): the "to hell with that" quote, a melted watch, a wet handkerchief.
