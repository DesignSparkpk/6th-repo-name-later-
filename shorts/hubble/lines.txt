He stares at a hotel shower head... and saves a one-point-five-billion-dollar telescope.
You see, in 1990, NASA launches Hubble. The first picture comes back... completely blurry.
Its giant mirror is off by one-fiftieth of a human hair.
Comedians roast it. A senator calls it a "techno-turkey."
And it gets worse: it's already in orbit. Nobody can climb inside to fix it.
Then engineer Jim Crocker checks into a German hotel.
In the shower, he watches the shower head slide on a rail... and tilt.
He freezes. "Mirrors... on little arms."
NASA builds tiny mirrors with the same flaw in reverse. Glasses. For a telescope.
Three years later, astronauts risk five spacewalks to install them. One slip, and Hubble is dead.
They slide them in. The little arms unfold.
Back on Earth, the first photo loads... razor sharp.
And the senator who called it a turkey holds it up for the cameras:
"The trouble with Hubble... is over."
