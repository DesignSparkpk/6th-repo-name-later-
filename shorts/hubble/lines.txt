He stares at a hotel shower head... and saves a billion-dollar telescope.
You see, in 1990, NASA launches Hubble, the sharpest eye ever sent into space.
The first picture comes down... blurry.
Its giant mirror is ground wrong, by one fiftieth of a human hair.
The world laughs. TV comedians mock it, and a senator calls it a "techno-turkey!"
And it gets worse. It's in orbit. Nobody can climb inside to fix that mirror.
So engineer Jim Crocker flies to Munich for a meeting.
In his hotel shower, the shower head slides up and down a rail... and tilts.
He freezes. "Mirrors... on little arms!"
Tiny mirrors, shaped with the same mistake in reverse, that unfold in front of the instruments. Glasses. For a telescope.
The papers laugh: "NASA's telescope needs glasses!"
Three years later, space shuttle Endeavour blasts off with the glasses on board.
Five spacewalks. Astronauts pull out an old instrument, slide the box in... and the little arms unfold.
Back on Earth, the first new picture loads, line by line...
Every star, razor sharp.
And the senator who called it a turkey? She holds the new picture up for the cameras.
The trouble with Hubble... is over.
