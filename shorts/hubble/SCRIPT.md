## 5. Facts and sources
- Hubble launched April 1990 (cost about $1.5 billion); the first images were blurred by spherical aberration: the primary mirror's edge was off by about 1/50 the width of a human hair. (NASA, Servicing Mission 1 pages; Astronomy magazine, "Techno-turkey: Remembering Hubble's vision troubles, 30 years on", 2020)
- Senator Barbara Mikulski branded it a "techno-turkey" (Astronomy magazine 2020; her own recollection, WTOP 2015). TV comedians joked about it (e.g. a late-night "Top Ten" list).
- James (Jim) Crocker had the idea for COSTAR in a hotel shower in Munich: the European shower head slid on a vertical rail and tilted, suggesting mirrors on arms that could be moved into the light path. The design was worked out by a team. (MIT News, "Rescuing the Hubble Space Telescope", 2013; Wikipedia, COSTAR)
- COSTAR redirected light to three of Hubble's instruments with mirrors that cancel the mirror's error; it replaced the High Speed Photometer. (NASA, SM1; Wikipedia, COSTAR)
- STS-61, Space Shuttle Endeavour, launched 2 December 1993 (a night launch), 11 days, five spacewalks, a record at the time; COSTAR was installed on spacewalk 4 by Thomas Akers and Kathryn Thornton. (NASA History, "30 Years Ago: STS-61"; NASA SM1)
- 13 January 1994: at NASA Goddard, Mikulski held up before-and-after pictures of a star; "'The trouble with Hubble is over,' she exclaimed."
  (Astronomical Society of the Pacific newsletter, Winter 1994; also recalled by Mikulski, Roll Call, and a 2004 Maryland report).
- Dramatized: "Mirrors... on little arms." (his eureka moment, our words). "One slip, and Hubble is dead." is the narrator's stakes line
  (the repair was a high-risk, never-tried job), not a claim about an event. The TV host and the senator are generic characters, not portraits.
