# The Man Buried in a Pringles Can: script v1 (~135 spoken words, ~40 s)

Why this topic: vidIQ check (2026-11-27). The hook "buried in a Pringles can" is proven (5.0M, 4.5M, 3.7M and 1.0M views, the last two on small channels), but almost every
version is a bare 8-30 s text clip. Ours is a full story with a human beat (the Walgreens flavor debate) and a payoff nobody else tells (the UK court case).
Rejected: microwave/chocolate (max 30k views, saturated), airplane window hole (two giants, the rest 30-50k).

## Facts and sources (verified 2026-11-27)
- Fredric J. Baur, the P&G food technician who designed the can, died in 2008 aged 89 (just under his 90th birthday). His children buried some of his ashes in a Pringles can; the rest went in a
  traditional urn (not all of the ashes fit). (TIME, "The Man Buried in a Pringles Can", 4 Jun 2008; Today I Found Out, 2012.)
- The family stopped at Walgreens on the way to the funeral home to buy the can. Larry Baur: "My siblings and I briefly debated what flavor to use, but I said, 'Look, we need to use the original.'" (TIME; Today I Found Out.)
- UK tax case: the High Court (Warren J, 2008) held Regular Pringles are not "made from the potato" within the VAT Act, so not "potato crisps". The Court of Appeal (20 May 2009, Revenue & Customs v Procter & Gamble UK, [2009] EWCA Civ 407)
  reversed it: with about 42% potato content they are crisps and standard-rated for VAT. (UK National Archives case-law pages; Marketing Week; HMRC VAT manual VFOOD8060.)
- NOT used (not confirmed): the "£100m" figure (a press estimate of back tax, P&G disputed it); that "all" his ashes went in the can (they did not); any specific day of death.
- "Once you pop, you can't stop" is the brand's slogan, used as a joke. The closing line "and he never did" is ours (dramatized).
- Dramatized: the lab scene, the car and the aisle gag, the courtroom chip-as-evidence. Baur's children are generic characters, not portraits.
- Rights: no brand logos or mascot. The tube is a generic red can with a plain white label and a stylised "chip" on it.
