# Second reference channel: covers of the latest 10 shorts (2026-11-27)
Only the cover frames could be studied: YouTube blocks downloads from the cloud server and vidIQ had 1 credit.
Motion, timing and colour statistics still need the video files.
Views: 769K to 31M (median ~8.7M). Titles follow "Why X ..." / "How X ..." / "The X That ..." with one paradox per title.

## What every cover shares (it is the same visual style as the first reference channel)
- **Characters:** pure white round heads with no skin tone. Tiny deadpan eyes (dots or half-lidded) and one small mouth.
  Very long thin ink legs (about 2x the torso). Narrow torsos, never a big egg body.
  Costume detail that tells the role at a glance: suspenders, neckerchief, train cap, bowler hat, police badge and moustache,
  top hat with rose and double-breasted buttons, ranger hat, astronaut patches, period robes.
  Head props: cap, helmet, hat on almost every character.
- **World:** much more realistic 3D than our toon boxes. Real-looking grass blades, stone walls with depth, PBR wood,
  metal and gravel, and proper sky. Every frame is crowded with story props (track crate and calendar, vault door,
  money pile and sports car, mine lamp and bucket, roller-coaster scaffolding).
- **Light and mood:** each place has its own mood: night blue with neon (pink prison, space station), warm interior lamp
  (tent, mine), golden sun shafts (castle), bright noon (prairie). Strong light shafts and glows, dark vignette edges.
- **Framing:** a 2-3 character medium shot, low eye-level camera, characters 40-60 % of the frame height, the subject
  centred and props framing both sides.
- **Captions:** one white word at a time (our rule stays: no captions).

## Gaps for our renders
1. Our heads were skin-toned and the bodies were big eggs (fixed in v2: white faces, ink limbs, clothing styles).
2. Our sets are low-poly and sparse. We need dense props per beat and better materials (grass, stone, metal).
3. Only one light mood in Death Ray v1. We need a mood per place.
